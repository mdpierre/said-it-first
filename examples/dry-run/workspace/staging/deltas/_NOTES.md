  transcript took less effort than triaging the reader notes. On a real
  archive, go by the Position statements and open only the cited stems.
- **Voice files add nothing here.** Every voice line was already in a read
  transcript; there are no previously reviewed conversations yet (ledger
  empty). On the next run, grep `voice/<year>.md` for founder/company/
  "mine"/"want" to find the Was side of anything new.
- **Short-quote trap.** Verify checks quotes of 40+ characters only.
  On this run, two short phrases in quotation marks were the assistant's words
  (a question and a one-word gloss). Grep for `"` on non-blockquote lines and
  check each one by hand.
- **Branch duplicates.** Cite the parent, never the `Branch ·` file.
- **Multi-day conversations.** Transcripts have no per-turn timestamps.
  Only the first and last turns can be dated (create_time, and create_time +
  span_hours). Middle turns are "conversation started <date>".
- **How Sam writes when thinking:** opens with "I keep ..." ("I keep telling
  myself", "I keep making plans", "The line I keep coming back to"). That
  phrasing marks a recurring position worth chasing. "Okay I slept on it" /
  "Coming back to this a week later" mark a turn that revises the one before.

  repeated line: in this archive, one was a real echo (borrowed-ambition), one
